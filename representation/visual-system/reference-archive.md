## §1 — Concept-first archive

### Concept 1 — Typographic multi-voice editorial system

**Universal concept.** Typography is an organizational system, not decoration. Each typeface owns a register-function; the combination creates a multi-voice publication where the typeface IS the wayfinding signal before the reader reads a word.

**Examples that prove the climb at world-class scale.**

- **Quanta Magazine** — Noe Display + Pangram Sans + Merriweather. The 3-typeface editorial archetype: display serif + neutral nav sans + reading serif. Built by Dogstudio (visual) + Barrel (dev); register = science as civic institution. *Cited in cascade source Section III.*
- **NYT Magazine 2026** — custom Cheltenham (slab serif retained from prior identity) + Franklin Gothic revival + custom slab. Bespoke type that rhymes with institutional history reads as authority; wholly novel type reads as rebrand. Designers: Gail Bichler + Ben Grandgenett + Henrik Kubel/A2-Type. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*
- **The Baffler Pentagram** (Eddie Opara + Megan Bowker) — assortment of typefaces per content category (Salvos / Outbursts / Poems each get their own font). Multi-voice extends beyond 3 when content categories warrant. *Cited in cascade source Section II.*
- **The New Republic Pentagram** (Eddie Opara) — 10 display typefaces for 10 editorial voices. Extreme multi-voice at principal-grade scale; experimental serif wordmark (IKANSEEYOUALL by Swiss Typefaces) occupies a third register beyond classic-institutional and tech-friendly: *intellectually dangerous*. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*
- **Sotheby's Pentagram** (Abbott Miller) — Mercury (custom; transitional high-contrast serif). Total system coherence (website + catalogues + magazine + signage + auction paddles) as a single civic object. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*

**Prime's expression.** A 5-typeface system with strict containment governance. Containment IS the cohesion mechanism — no typeface escapes its scope.

| Typeface slot | Register-function | Containment scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** (display serif) | Editorial authority — masthead, district names, canto headings, evergreen civic-publication identity | DISpatch-locked. |
| **Body** (reading serif) | Reading intimacy — article body, footnotes, pull-quotes, long-form sanctuary | DISpatch-locked. |
| **Nav** (humanist sans) | Navigation clarity — ChapterRail, SiteNav, CosmologyMarker, Prime platform wayfinding | Prime-platform-wide. Carries identity across Buildings. |
| **Meta/code** (monospace or technical-grotesque) | META content silhouette — code blocks, JSON/YAML, DLDS panels, terminal-search, peer-behind-curtain content | Component-locked. |
| **Civic/Dante** (medieval-touched display) | Asset signature — cartography labels, district names on the map, in-asset typography | Asset-locked. NYC green street-sign feel ("burned in"). |

Specific typeface selection per slot: forward to CD2 + CD4 with adversarial APCA / perceptual-uniformity validation per pairing. Slot-based triage in §2 below.

### Concept 2 — Hierarchical-layered grid (grid as architectural document)

**Universal concept.** The column grid is structural, not decorative. It maps directly to information architecture and reads as a load-bearing wall, not a responsive-breakpoint accident.

**Examples that prove the climb.**

- **NYT Magazine 2026** — 7-column with dedicated narrow caption column. The narrow caption column is *load-bearing wall*: invisible but structural, creating visual breathing room without wasted space. *Cited in cascade source Section I + Section IV (Move 2).*
- **The New Republic Pentagram** — 12-column grid for full structural flexibility; photography becomes evidence not decoration. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*
- **The Baffler Pentagram** — per-section grid flexibility for editorial differentiation; "more flexibility to use illustration and photography at full structural weight, not decorative supplement." *Cited in cascade source Section II.*

**Prime's expression.** A hierarchical-layered grid — modular but irregular — where the cartography occupies its own active grid layer (dynamic participant, not background) and a simpler hierarchical grid sits on top for site design. The two layers marry at coordinate-handoff points: cartography-grid coordinates feed site-grid placement; site-grid layout respects cartography-grid semantics. Layers can evolve independently within the marriage.

This is the design-behind-the-design DNA-carries-through principle. Future Buildings express different surface registers (a Building might look like a blog; another might look like a video game; another might look like a dashboard) on the same grid substrate. The DNA carries through structure, not surface.

The hierarchical-layered grid softens cartography-coupling problems identified in V3 by giving designs columns to design AROUND, not borders for freeform layout.

Specific grid implementation: forward to CD3 (cartography-grid) + CD4 (site-grid) + CD3+CD4 boundary (coordinate-handoff API).

### Concept 3 — Metro-map wayfinding + automation engine

**Universal concept.** Page furniture is civic wayfinding — page numbers, column names, dates, institutional anchors function like a city's street signs. *Where in the building / city are you reading?* visible at a glance.

**Examples that prove the climb.**

- **NYT Magazine 2026** — page furniture migrated to top margin (bottom space opens for content to breathe). *Cited in cascade source Section I + Section IV (Move 3).*
- **The Atlantic Pentagram** (Peter Mendelsund + Oliver Munday) — vertical information band aligned with the A monogram. Civic navigation rail equivalent of a city directory listing; functional and elegant simultaneously. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*
- **The Baffler Pentagram** — per-section typography as wayfinding (typography signals content type before reading). *Cited in cascade source Section II.*

**Prime's expression.** Metro-map wayfinding — *you are here + neighbors + next stops* — replacing pyramid representation. The cosmology marker on every article surface tells the reader where they are AND where they can go from here AND who their neighbors are. Wayfinding is graphics-only at the article level; no Italian-in-text overlay (DP-Q2 closed via metro-map per master plan §11). Article log-dating system from V3 stays at article header for chronological wayfinding; metro-map asset handles spatial-cosmological wayfinding.

**Automation-engine commitment.** The page-furniture system is machine-composable, not session-to-session authored. Per master plan §3.A semantic-componentization gap (NYT Magazine 2026 + WaPo SND46 + Pudding SND46 + Pentagram redesigns precedent): brand typographic rules + spatial relationships + accessibility standards encoded as machine-readable libraries that AI agents (or dynamic rendering engines) compose layouts from semantic content. At multipurpose-platform scope, human-only-verification does not scale; semantic componentization makes scale possible without losing principal-grade design quality.

Specific component shape + automation-engine spec: forward to CD3 (cartographic encoding of metro-map) + CD4 (component shape + spec.md authoring) + Stream-B implementation per §3.B.

### Concept 4 — Atmosphere as physical substrate

**Universal concept.** Atmosphere is the substrate the publication is printed on; it carries warmth and weight without calling attention to itself. Spectacle (WebGL, dark fields, glow effects) is a different valid register for different surfaces; this microsite climbs toward atmosphere.

**Examples that prove the climb.**

- **The Paris Review Pentagram** (Matt Willey) — recycled paper warmth ("physically softer and visually yellower" stock). Source: Issue No. 56 (1973); neo-vintage minimalist. Klim Type Foundry Founders Grotesk + Heldane Text. Smaller trim size = intimacy ("more like reading a novel and less like reading a magazine"). *Cited in cascade source Section II + Section IV (Move 4).*
- **INQUE Magazine** (Matt Willey + Dan Crowe) — print-only annual; typewriter body + graphic bold headline; "light and dark as page design." Eastern European Modernist aesthetic. *Cited in cascade source Section II.*
- **The Baffler Pentagram** — open space + visuals breathe; "design that helps organize the journal's creative anarchy." *Cited in cascade source Section II.*

**Prime's expression.** D3.js contour + P5.js paper grain atmospheric layer reads as the physical substrate the publication is printed on, not as an effect layer.

Atmospheric register concept-family (currently inclined): paper grain, warm-white scale, contour-ink, ambient warmth.

Atmospheric chrome anchor concepts (currently inclined; spatial-perceptual vocabulary; tokens ratify in CD2):

| Concept | Spatial-perceptual reference |
|---|---|
| *Upper-stratum* | Warmest, lightest — printed paper held to natural light |
| *Mid-stratum* | Warm-white in ambient room lighting |
| *Atmospheric reflectivity* | Subtle warm-tone wash; printed-paper substrate's micro-reflection of incident light |
| *Editorial warmth* | Warm yellow shift of incandescent room light printed on warm paper |

Token names currently inclined: `sky-high` / `sky-low` / `reflect` / `window-warm`.

**Other registers prove different concepts at scale and serve different surface needs.** Self-illumination registers (neon-glow, synthwave, cyberpunk); reflective-surface registers (glass-morphism, mirror-sheen); luminance-inverted registers (dark-mode-with-glow). Each is valid for its purpose. Future Prime Buildings choose per purpose — a future Prime app might inline glassmorphic icons; a future Prime nightlife microsite might express red-light-district register; both valid Prime expressions in their register.

- **NYT Magazine 2026** — within-system weight + tracking + typeface differentiation across sections. Multi-voice through bespoke type-system articulation. *Cited in cascade source Section I.*

### Concept 7 — Walking metaphor + parallel-native digital-print

**Universal concept.** The publication does not compromise digital for print or print for digital — it develops parallel-native formats where each is a complete expression of the same identity. The architecture of the publication IS the marketing of the publication (Burj Khalifa precedent: architecture and design WERE the marketing).

**Examples that prove the climb.**

- **NYT Magazine 2026** — system works in both print and digital without compromise; parallel-native formats. *Cited in cascade source Section IV (Move 7).*
- **ZEIT Online** — two-mode architecture (homepage as navigation; article as sanctuary); Franziska only on article pages; "won't find Franziska on the homepage or landing pages. As a text typeface, it tends to stay in the background." *Cited in cascade source Section III.*
- **The Pudding** — each article as its own designed object; per-article-as-self-contained-visual-system; "the site function as both a magazine and a gallery of creative coding." SND46 World's Best Designed Digital co-winner. *Cited in cascade source Section III.*
- **The New Yorker** — magazine glossary at homepage top (feature story / things to read / ticker / coming-soon); guides reader through the magazine's editorial rhythm. *(Mayor 2026-05-08 cited as the only editorial homepage he relatively enjoys.)*

**Prime's expression.** A six-level cosmological-cartographic walking metaphor:

> Prime City → districts → buildings (DISpatch is one) → reception (DISpatch's homepage) → offices (META and NARRATIVE production units inside DISpatch) → missives (articles output FROM the building's offices)

The article is not the building. The article is the missive from the building. The magazine, the dispatch itself, is the building. The offices are the production departments inside it. The citizens working in the offices author the missives.

**V1 ship shape.** Building-and-below stack: reception (homepage) + offices (production units; META + NARRATIVE departments) + missives (articles). City + districts forward-pointed (Prime City's homepage does not exist yet; other Buildings do not exist yet).

**Reception ↔ offices ↔ missives.** The homepage IS the building's reception/storefront/glossary. It carries the magazine's glossary at the top — feature story, things to read today, ticker (live updates from Prime), coming-soon articles, live commit-tickers, Virgil chatbot, district map, building layout. The reader walks the path, sees what is happening in the offices, then clicks an article and sees the result of what is happening in those offices. The walk-through experience is the home page; the article is the missive.

The article surface treats every article URL as a self-contained, designed, citable magazine page (per master plan §3.B Astro Server Islands). The article IS the missive — designed as its own object, not a template skin.

**Two-mode architecture: porous gradient.** Homepage-level affordances (ChapterRail, CosmologyMarker, metro-map "you are here") cross into the article surface to preserve civic-wayfinding from the city's map onto the building's interior. Strict article-as-sanctuary affordances (no SiteNav search input on article surface; reading-progress affordance; positionally-controlled cross-post buttons) preserve the reading register. CD4 spec.md per-component authoring controls the dilution by spec'ing exactly which affordances cross modes.

Specific component-mode-spec authoring: forward to CD3 (cartography expression of city → districts → buildings) + CD4 (reception-and-offices component layout) + CD5 (walking-through motion register) + CD6 (cross-surface cohesion).
